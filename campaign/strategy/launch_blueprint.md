# XAIHT Launch Blueprint

## Executive recommendation

Launch XAIHT around a category that the current product can credibly own:

> **Human-commanded AI operations for builders who need software to touch the real world.**

Tlamatini should not enter the market as another code autocomplete tool or another generic workflow canvas. Her strongest commercial position is the intersection of five proven surfaces:

1. Local-first project intelligence and hybrid retrieval.
2. Human-gated agentic execution with visible, editable workflows.
3. Direct operation across software, desktop tools, browsers, creative engines, and embedded hardware.
4. Delegation to external coding agents through ACPX and MCP.
5. Plain-Python extensibility that keeps operating logic readable and under user control.

This position creates a credible wedge between coding agents, automation platforms, and industrial/creative toolchains.

## Priority audiences

### 1. Technical founders and small engineering teams

Need: one operator that can understand a codebase, execute bounded work, preserve context, and turn successful actions into repeatable workflows.

Proof to lead with: hybrid RAG, Multi-Turn, Ask Execs, Step-by-Step, Create Flow, deterministic file tools, External MCP, ACPX.

### 2. Embedded, robotics, and maker teams

Need: a bridge from natural-language intent to build, flash, observe, and iterate loops without losing operator control.

Proof to lead with: STM32er, ESP32er, ESPHomer, Arduiner, Camcorder, Video-Analyzer, Talker, Whisperer.

### 3. Creative technology and simulation studios

Need: automation that spans code, scenes, assets, and repeatable production steps.

Proof to lead with: direct Unreal Engine control, one-prompt Unreal Engine 5.8 C++ scaffolding, Blender control, image interpretation, video analysis.

### 4. Security-aware IT and operations teams

Need: local project context, inspectable agent code, permission gates, controlled connectors, and clear operational boundaries.

Proof to lead with: local app and RAG, Ask Execs, External MCP Doctor, security-audit skill, Nmapper authorization language, plain-Python agent jurisdiction.

### 5. Education, labs, and emerging-market innovation hubs

Need: a reusable, model-flexible operating layer that can run on accessible hardware and teach automation as inspectable code rather than a closed black box.

Proof to lead with: MIT license, source availability, local models, readable agents, visual workflows, skills, device and creative-engine breadth.

## Message hierarchy

### Supporting pillars

- **Knows the work:** project-aware retrieval, source context, and self-knowledge.
- **Acts with boundaries:** Multi-Turn execution, approvals, visible tool evidence, and hard cancellation.
- **Touches real systems:** Unreal Engine, Blender, STM32, ESP32, ESPHome, Arduino, browser, desktop, voice, and media.

### Do not say

- Do not say Tlamatini is autonomous without stating operator controls and authorization.
- Do not claim enterprise certifications, revenue, customers, market share, or benchmark leadership.
- Do not claim all processing is local. The application and retrieval stack are local-first, while configured LLMs or connectors may be cloud services.
- Do not claim the newest release is `v1.40.1`; the live release resolves to `v1.41.3`.
- Do not repeat the stale `75 tools` total; the current inventory is 94 Multi-Turn tools.
- Do not repeat old 82-agent copy; the live inventory is 85.
- Do not use changing third-party subscription prices as the core value proposition.

## Offer design

The MIT core remains free. Commercial value should come from services and trust, not artificial scarcity.

### Launch-now offers

- **Tlamatini Community:** MIT source and public releases.
- **XAIHT Launch Lab:** paid installation, model setup, workflow design, and operator training.
- **Certified Workflow Pack:** paid design and validation of customer-specific `.flw` workflows and agent configurations.
- **Embedded and Creative Integration Sprint:** paid Unreal, Blender, STM32, ESP32, ESPHome, or Arduino implementation support.
- **Operational Readiness Review:** paid configuration, permission, connector, credential-scope, and recovery review.

### Roadmap offers - never present as shipped

- Signed enterprise release channel and long-term support.
- Central policy, audit, and fleet management.
- Organization identity and role-based access controls.
- Managed connector catalog and certified agent marketplace.
- Commercial support SLAs and regulated-industry deployment packages.

## Launch sequence

### Phase 0 - Proof before promotion, days 0-14

- Align website and campaign on `v1.41.3`, 85 agents, 94 Multi-Turn tools, and 27 skills.
- Publish the claims ledger and security/responsibility boundary.
- Package three reproducible hero demos: Unreal/Blender, embedded robotic loop, and project-to-workflow automation.
- Capture proof artifacts: screen recordings, workflow files, build logs, and final outputs.

### Phase 1 - Category introduction, days 15-30

- Release the commercial identity and campaign gallery.
- Publish a founder narrative: why XAIHT exists and why human command is the product principle.
- Launch the refreshed website with one primary call to action: run Tlamatini or request a Launch Lab engagement.
- Publish the investor deck and a concise public technical brief derived from the impact paper.

### Phase 2 - Demonstration campaign, days 31-60

- Run a weekly proof series: Build, Move, Create, Connect, Govern.
- Pair every visual ad with a reproducible workflow or repository link.
- Recruit 10 design partners across embedded systems, creative technology, and small engineering teams.
- Track time-to-first-success, workflow reuse, intervention rate, and retained weekly use.

### Phase 3 - Commercial validation, days 61-120

- Convert design partners into paid Launch Lab or integration engagements.
- Publish case studies only after written approval and measured outcomes.
- Define the first supportable enterprise package from repeated customer needs.
- Use measured support burden and activation data to decide where managed services are justified.

### Phase 4 - Scale decision, days 121-180

- Select one primary vertical wedge based on revenue and retained use.
- Build the enterprise roadmap around observed blockers, not generic checklists.
- Prepare a fundraise using measured activation, retention, services revenue, and verified customer evidence.

## Launch metrics

### Product

- Installer completion rate.
- Time to first successful bounded action.
- Time to first saved `.flw` workflow.
- Weekly retained operators.
- Successful runs per active operator.
- Human intervention and denial rates.
- Hard-cancel recovery success.

### Commercial

- Qualified Launch Lab requests.
- Design partners activated.
- Paid conversion rate.
- Services revenue and gross margin.
- Average implementation time.
- Expansion from one use case to a second workflow.

### Trust

- Security issues by severity.
- Credential-scope findings before deployment.
- Unauthorized-target attempts safely refused.
- Reproducible demos with complete evidence.
- Public claims corrected within one release cycle.

## Investor framing

The investable thesis is not that XAIHT has already won a horizontal market. It is that Tlamatini demonstrates a credible, unusually broad operator layer, and that the next company-building step is to turn technical breadth into a narrow, repeatable commercial wedge. The pitch should therefore emphasize:

- Working product depth and integration breadth.
- Human-commanded operating model as a durable trust position.
- Open-source distribution with paid implementation and enterprise trust layers.
- A disciplined plan to discover the highest-value vertical rather than fabricated traction.
- A scenario-based capital plan clearly labeled as an assumption.
